One API for a network of 20 lenders

I built Ping3 from scratch so partners could show offers from several lenders inside their own application flow. Ping3 took on the integration work for each lender. In the July sample, each application generated 4.8 lender requests on average. Of 2,112 applications, 369 received at least one approval.

What partners no longer have to build

Each lender requires different information and returns its decision in a different format. A partner integrating directly would have to maintain another connection for every lender it added. I built a separate platform to take on that work. Yandex was among the partners we integrated with. The partner submits one application. Ping3 prepares requests for eligible lenders and returns a standard set of offers.

The application specification covers 50 fields. Of these, 28 are mandatory at intake. The rest are conditional or optional. For each offer the partner receives an amount, term, rate, application link and approval type. It can distinguish a preliminary approval from a full one before the customer opens the offer.

What happened to 2,112 applications

This sample covers short-term loans in July 2026. Applications arrived on 23 days that month. Several lenders could review the same application, so conversion looks different from a lender’s perspective and a customer’s.

  • 2,112applications received
  • 10,062requests to lenders
  • 464lender approvals
  • 369applications with an approval
  1. 01How often Ping3 sent a result

    Ping3 sent a processing result for 2,110 of 2,112 applications (99.9%). A result could contain an empty list of offers.

  2. 02How many requests each application generated

    10,062 requests across 2,112 applications works out to 4.8 requests per application. The network size describes potential reach. This average describes the July sample.

  3. 03How often there was something to offer

    Lenders approved 464 of 10,062 requests (4.6%). At the application level, 369 of 2,112 received at least one approval (17.5%). That is the share for which the partner could offer the customer a loan.

  4. 04How far the data follows the customer

    The report recorded 255 clicks through to offers. Counting funded loans requires a separate message from the lender identifying the offer. The July data follows the customer as far as the click.

Why sending more requests can miss the point

In July, 5,227 of 10,062 requests went to lenders whose criteria did not cover the applicant’s profile (about 52%). Another 2,948 were declined after scoring. The first group gives me a reason to revisit lender matching. In the second, the lender had assessed the application and made a credit decision.

The report also tracked technical losses. Its recorded rate was 21.8% on 14–16 July and 5.2% on 29–30 July, a difference of 16.6 percentage points. Connection failures and data-format errors were addressed during that period. The figures do not isolate the effect of any one fix.

How I choose payment providers

I also own payment processing. A provider’s completion rate can vary across legal entities, so I select providers for each entity separately and regularly check whether the preferred provider still performs better.

A useful comparison needs similar payment attempts over the same period. I look first at the share the bank authorises, then at how many of those authorisations become completed payments. Two routes can have similar overall success rates and still lose payments at different steps.

Proposed product test

When is another lender worth adding?

Who would benefit?

I would start with applicants who rarely receive an offer from the current network. A lender willing to serve those profiles gives the integration a clear purpose.

How I would measure its contribution

I would randomly split eligible applications between the existing network and that same network with the new lender. The primary measure would be the share with at least one confirmed funded loan within the same observation window.

That test depends on comparable funding reports from lenders. A faster reporting cycle could otherwise make one lender look more valuable before the remaining outcomes arrive.
